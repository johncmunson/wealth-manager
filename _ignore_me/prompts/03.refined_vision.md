# Agentic Brokerage Vision

Working Product Name: "Wealth Manager"

## Product Thesis

We are building a **web-first, agent-native brokerage for competent long-term investors who want to delegate portfolio management work without being buried in traditional brokerage complexity**.

This is not “Robinhood with a chatbot.” It is closer to:

> **ChatGPT-style agent workspace + brokerage execution + long-term portfolio strategy + automation.**

The product surface should be radically minimal, calm, and high-trust — closer in spirit to Apple or Vercel than a traditional brokerage terminal. The underlying capabilities, however, should be deep and powerful because the wealth manager agent is capable.

Tagline:

> **An AI-powered brokerage that helps you build and maintain a long-term portfolio without micromanaging every trade.**

---

## Target User

The initial user is someone who is:

- Already comfortable with ChatGPT, copilots, agents, and agentic interfaces.
- An intermediate to advanced long-term investor.
- Short on time.
- Not an absolute beginner.
- Not looking for active-trading tools or a feature-bloated brokerage.
- Interested in a streamlined, minimal, high-trust wealth management experience.

They want the agent to help them think, decide, execute, maintain, and automate — without forcing them through a large collection of traditional brokerage screens.

---

## Core Product Philosophy

The product should default and steer toward:

- Simple portfolios.
- Boring durability.
- Diversification.
- Long-term discipline.
- Tax awareness where relevant.
- Dollar-based investing language where appropriate.
- Minimal cognitive load.

At the same time, the product should support sophisticated users and more complex portfolio strategies when needed.

The product should be **opinionated, but not paternalistic**.

---

## Core UX Model

The app is **chat-first**.

A returning user should land in a **new chat**, similar to ChatGPT. Users can have many conversation threads. Threads may naturally become task-oriented, but they should not be rigidly constrained.

The conversation is the primary workspace, but it is not plaintext-only. The wealth manager agent should be able to render rich interactive artifacts inline, including:

- Strategy drafts.
- Portfolio allocation views.
- Charts.
- Tables.
- Forms.
- Order previews.
- Approval dialogs.
- Automation cards.
- Research briefs.
- Portfolio reviews.
- Receipts.

Traditional pages may exist, but they support the conversational workflow rather than replacing it.

Guiding rule:

> **Pages expose durable state. Chat is where work happens.**

---

## Core Persistent Objects

The most important durable product objects are:

### 1. Conversations

Conversations are the primary workspace.

- Users can have many conversation threads.
- Conversation branching is a non-goal for MVP.
- Threads should be auto-titled around the work being done when possible.
- Conversation compaction should be supported.

Compaction requirements:

- Support both auto-compaction and manual compaction.
- Whenever compaction happens, insert a discreet horizontal marker into the chat history.
- Users should always be able to see the percentage of the context window currently filled.
- Manual compaction should be exposed as a traditional UI affordance, not as something the user must ask the agent to do.
- Manual compaction should allow custom compaction instructions.
- Users do not need to see the generated compaction summary.

Important financial state should not live only in conversation memory. Durable outcomes should be committed to first-class product objects such as Strategy, Automations, Activity, and Portfolio.

### 2. Strategy

Strategy is a first-class product object.

- There is one primary Strategy per brokerage account.
- The Strategy is created and edited through the agent, at least initially.
- Direct editing can come later.
- Strategy acts as the north star for portfolio construction, trading, rebalancing, recurring investing, and automation.

At a product level, Strategy may include:

- Investment goal.
- Time horizon.
- Risk posture.
- Target allocation.
- Approved asset universe.
- Excluded assets, sectors, or preferences.
- Core vs satellite posture.
- Contribution plan.
- Rebalancing philosophy.
- Tax posture.
- Links to related Automations.
- Plain-English rationale.
- Revision history.

The exact Strategy schema is intentionally deferred. It may eventually be structured, semi-structured, or a hybrid.

### 3. Portfolio

Portfolio represents the current brokerage account state.

It should include:

- Account value.
- Value over time.
- Holdings.
- Allocation.
- Cash.
- Drift from Strategy.
- Recent relevant changes.

The Portfolio page should be mostly passive, with chat-native calls to action such as:

- “Review this with the agent.”
- “Explain this drift.”
- “Draft a rebalance.”
- “Invest idle cash.”
- “Analyze this holding.”

### 4. Automations

Automations are delegated recurring or background behaviors.

They are agent-native from day one:

- To create an automation, tell the agent.
- To edit an automation, tell the agent.
- To inspect automations, ask the agent or use the Automations surface.

Automations may include:

- Recurring investing / dollar-cost averaging.
- Rebalance checks.
- Drift-based alerts or actions.
- Later, tax-loss harvesting.

Different automations may support different autonomy modes, such as:

- Notify-only.
- Approval-required.
- Act-then-notify.

The exact approval and autonomy model is intentionally deferred, along with the degree of customization that will potentially be exposed to the user.

### 5. Activity

Activity is the trust and audit surface.

It should include:

- Orders.
- Trades.
- Deposits and withdrawals.
- Automation runs.
- Strategy changes.
- Approvals.
- Agent actions.
- Human-readable receipts.

Every meaningful action should leave behind a clear record of what happened and why.

## Product Surface and Navigation

The product should feel like a chat app with supporting pages.

A ChatGPT-like layout is appropriate, with navigation for:

- New Chat.
- Conversations.
- Portfolio.
- Strategy.
- Automations.
- Activity.
- Funding / Account.

The app should remain minimal. Supporting pages should not become large traditional workflows unless necessary. They should expose state and provide entry points back into agent-native workflows.

---

## Onboarding and Account States

The product should use a hybrid onboarding path.

Users should be able to explore value before completing full brokerage setup:

### 1. Exploration

Before opening or funding a brokerage account, users can:

- Chat with the agent.
- Draft a Strategy.
- Analyze pasted holdings.
- Explore hypothetical portfolios.
- Preview what implementation could look like.

No real trades can be executed in this state.

### 2. Brokerage Opened, Unfunded

Once the brokerage account is opened but unfunded, users can:

- Maintain an active Strategy.
- Receive funding prompts.
- Preview orders.

Execution still requires funding.

> Note: "user account created" and "brokerage account opened" might actually be one and the same, in which case this state is largely the same as the first one.

### 3. Funded

Once funded, users can:

- Place trades.
- Rebalance.
- Use Automations.
- Maintain and monitor their Portfolio against Strategy.

The empty state should start with intent, not forms:

> “Tell me what you’re investing for, and I’ll draft a long-term Strategy.”

## Investment Scope

MVP supports a subset of Alpaca-supported assets:

- Stocks.
- ETFs.
- Bonds or bond exposure where supported.

If individual bonds are supported by Alpaca, they should be considered in scope. Otherwise, bond exposure can be represented through bond ETFs, Treasury ETFs, or cash-like instruments.

Explicitly out of scope:

- Crypto.
- Options/futures.
- IPOs.
- Advanced active-trading surfaces.
- Broad Alpaca feature coverage for its own sake.

The product should support complex portfolios and strategies, but the default should steer toward simple, durable, diversified, tax-aware portfolios.

The Strategy should distinguish between:

- **Core portfolio**: diversified long-term allocation, usually ETF/bond-oriented.
- **Satellite positions**: individual stocks or narrower bets, optionally capped by Strategy rules.

---

## Trading Experience

Trading should be agent-native.

The user should be able to say things like:

- “Invest $10,000 according to my Strategy.”
- “Buy 17.483 shares of VTI.”
- “Rebalance my portfolio.”
- “Draft a limit order for AAPL.”

The product should default to dollar-based language where appropriate, but users must still be able to specify share quantities.

MVP should mostly support:

- Market orders.
- Simple limit orders.

Traditional order tickets and advanced trading affordances can come later.

Execution should flow through agent-generated previews and approval surfaces where appropriate. The exact approval policy is intentionally deferred.

---

## Research Experience

Market and security research should be agent-native.

For MVP, research should appear as inline artifacts such as:

- Research briefs.
- Comparison tables.
- Basic charts.
- Portfolio impact summaries.
- Risk summaries.

Standalone asset pages are not needed for MVP.

The research surface should support long-term investing decisions rather than become a full market terminal.

---

## Proactivity and Notifications

The product is not generally proactive by default.

Proactivity should happen through explicit Automations.

Notifications should be event-based, not news-based.

Notify for things like:

- Automation executed.
- Approval required.
- Deposit or withdrawal status changed.
- Order filled or failed.
- Rebalance check completed.
- Strategy change confirmed.

Avoid noisy generic alerts such as:

- Market commentary.
- News blasts.
- Stock hype.
- Unrequested macro commentary.

---

## External Portfolio Analysis

MVP does not need native transfer or migration capabilities.

However, the agent should be able to advise on an existing portfolio if the user provides holdings manually, for example by pasting them into chat.

First-class account transfer, ACAT, account linking, and migration workflows can come later.

---

## Business Model

The product should be free for now.

Pricing and monetization can be revisited later.

---

## Deferred Decisions

The following areas are intentionally not locked down yet:

- Exact Strategy schema vs semi-structured or unstructured representation.
- Approval policy details.
- User-configurable autonomy model.
- Prompt-level vs harness-level safety boundaries.
- Detailed compliance flows.
- Documents and tax document surfaces.
- Direct editing of Strategy or Automations outside the agent.
- Advanced transfer and migration support.
- Standalone asset pages.
- Traditional order ticket UI.
- Advanced trading workflows.
- Conversation branching.

---

## Current MVP Summary

Build a minimal, chat-first, agent-native brokerage where competent long-term investors can:

- Create one durable portfolio Strategy.
- Open and fund a taxable brokerage account.
- Execute trades through rich agent-generated previews.
- Monitor Portfolio value, allocation, cash, and drift over time.
- Delegate recurring Automations such as DCA and rebalancing checks.
- Review all meaningful actions through a clear Activity history.

The product should feel sparse and elegant on the surface, but powerful underneath.
